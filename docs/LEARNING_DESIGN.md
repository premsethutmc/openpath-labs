# Learning design

## The arc for every concept

1. **Meet it in a real machine.** Something works or fails and the learner wants to know why. *(The engine overheats and wears.)*
2. **Isolate it.** A focused playground for the one principle. *(A viscosity bench: drop a ball through oil, heat it, shear it.)*
3. **Own it.** Change everything; design something of your own. *(Blend an oil for hot and cold.)*
4. **Push it to the edge.** Put it back in the machine under hard scenarios; go beyond the model with the labelled AI helper. *(Four days parked in Alaska at −35 °C. Does it start? How long until oil pressure?)*

## Two depths, one world

| | Guided path | Open sandbox |
|---|---|---|
| For | Every student | Curious students, projects |
| Shape | 5–8 short steps per idea, each with one action | Everything editable, any scenario |
| Feedback | Explanation after each step | Live numbers, checks, notebook |
| Goal | Understand the subject | Discover, invent, build |

Both run on the same scene, so a guided learner can step into open mode at any point and come back.

## Pause and think

At turning points, ask one question before revealing the answer: *"The same bracket held 80 kg in steel. What will happen in aluminium?"* The learner commits a guess (more / same / less, or a number), then sees the result. Guesses are recorded.

## Assessment from actions

Faculty see what learners *did*: predictions and whether they were right, what they changed, which scenarios they tried, which designs failed and how they fixed them. Copying text from a chatbot does not produce this trail.

## Live equations

Every number on screen can be expanded into its equation with live values, e.g. `σ = M·y / I = (1.2 kN·m × 25 mm) / 0.52×10⁶ mm⁴ = 57.7 MPa`. Change the load and the numbers change in place.

## Language and access

English first; Tamil next (interface and guided text). Mobile first: one-handed use, 44 px touch targets, no sideways scrolling. Works offline once loaded.
