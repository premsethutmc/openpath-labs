What is simulated, and what is not

Simulated lab fixture, not manufacturer or field test data. The pump is Pump House’s model pump (Euler equation with slip and losses); the pipes use Darcy–Weisbach with Colebrook friction. Water at 32 °C; the basin surface is 4 m above the pump, so the suction is flooded and every case stays at least 2 m of NPSH away from cavitation.

The fault is an added local loss R·Q² either in the inlet strainer or in the discharge section (non-return valve + gate valve). In each case R is the same for both candidates, so flow, pump head, power and efficiency are identical whichever section is at fault — the dashboard alone cannot tell them apart.

The gauge reads the differential pressure between two taps either side of one section, on the same pipe, at equal elevation and diameter: it measures only that section’s loss, Δp = ρg(Kclean·V²/2g + fault). A clean section’s loss scales with flow² (turbulent flow).

Not modelled: partial blockage that changes with time, air in the line, gauge error, pump wear.
