A flat line means steady flow. A regular wave means vortices are peeling off alternately — its frequency gives the Strouhal number.

What is modelled, and what is not

Method: lattice Boltzmann (D2Q9, two-relaxation-time), which solves the Navier–Stokes equations for slow, nearly incompressible flow. No-slip walls by bounce-back; forces by momentum exchange.

Checked: channel flow matches the exact parabolic solution within 0.4 %; mass is conserved; the Schäfer–Turek cylinder benchmark (Re 100) gives the correct shedding frequency (St 0.297). See docs/VALIDATION.md.

Limits: two-dimensional; Reynolds numbers up to a few hundred. Real cars and aircraft run at Re in the millions, where flow is three-dimensional and turbulent — the patterns here teach the principles, not those exact numbers. Coefficients include tunnel-wall blockage; peak forces are ~15 % high on the live grid.
